The short version

How I got here

01

Houston, then the tanker

More than ten years in Houston oil and gas and maritime logistics, working up to VP. Part of that was as a cargo rep on a chemical tanker: the numbers, the paperwork, and the procedure. You do not guess when the cargo is the job.

02

Left the office

I left the corporate job for a hands-on life as a ranch hand in northern New Mexico. The fluorescent lights had a long run. The mountains are louder, in a good way.

03

The actual work

Elk season means hunters on the place, and I am in that work with them. The rest is off-grid systems: solar, spring boxes, cisterns, and pumps. If it makes power or moves water, it eventually wants a wrench.

04

Homesteading skills

The homesteading skills have arrived the same way the maritime ones did. Something needs doing, you learn it, and the next time your hands already know. I like that part.

05

Sally

Sally is a cream dog with one half-blue eye, and she is my copilot. Her sister is Sacha. The truck is better company with them than most rooms I used to sit in.

From the desk to the ranch

Habits that carried over

The job changed. These three did not.

  • Nothing moves without a sign-off.
  • The paper trail wins.
  • Automate the routine and escalate the weird.
